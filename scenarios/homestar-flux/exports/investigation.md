## Where we are

U2, the 3.3 V regulator, gets 4.86 V in, gives 2.07 V out, and stays cool at idle (R25, R28, R31): it is not feeding an overload, so U2 itself is failing. That leaves the microcontroller running near its lower limit, which explains every button resetting the unit. This round replaces U2 with hot air, checks the new one gives 3.3 V, and runs the original test, buttons and motor, several times.

**Current step:** Get a replacement 3.3 V regulator (`get-u2`)

## Findings

- **Failure mode: brownout reset.** Move and Shooting Star both make the microcontroller switch the motor output on. Something then pulls the supply down far enough to reset the microcontroller, which restarts with the lamp on and every function off.
- **Not the adapter.** Normal draw is about 1 A (5 W at 5 V), and a 4 A adapter changes nothing: the overload is inside the unit.
- **Both buttons go through the motor path.** Shooting stars only run while the dome rotates, and their mechanism depends on the motor turning.
- **Not the motor.** With the motor's `M+` wire disconnected, Shooting Star still resets the unit.
- **No visible damage.** On either side of the board, including close-ups of the motor driver area, and under a magnifier around U4: its pins, D10, D11, C6, C7, C9, R13 and the motor pads.
- **The motor driver is U4, a Mixic MX08L.** A single H-bridge in SOP-8. Its datasheet lists thermal shutdown but no short-circuit protection. With both inputs low its outputs are off, so a shorted output stage stays quiet at idle and only shorts the supply once the microcontroller drives an input, which matches the symptom.
- **Timer resets the unit too.** Timer does not use the motor, yet pressing it resets the unit like Move. The fault is not specific to the motor output: it follows a button press, or any small change of load.
- **Nothing on U4's side is shorted, power off.** U4's motor supply reads OL to ground, its outputs read megohms to ground and to the supply, OUTA and OUTB alike, and D10 and D11 are good diodes. A static pass does not clear U4 of a weak output (on-resistance), but nothing there explains a reset on Timer.
- **C7 is on U4's motor supply.** 0 Ω between U4 pin 4 and C7's `+` lead: that connection is now measured.
- **U4's supply is not wired straight to the DC+ pad.** 8.6 MΩ between U4 pin 4 and DC+ with the power off. Probably through the power switch S1, which may have been OFF: the switch position was not recorded.
- **D13 reads unlike its twin D12.** D12 reads 0.573 V forward and OL reverse, a good diode. D13, the same glass type and right beside the 3.3 V regulator U2, reads 0.356 V forward and 0.457 V reverse. D15 reads 0.859 V reverse and D16 0.569 V reverse. In circuit, a parallel path can lower both readings: D13 is suspect, and only lifting one end settles it.
- **Four resistors read their marked value.** R3 68 Ω, R12 1 kΩ, R17 10 kΩ, and R13 0 Ω (the link from OUTA to M-).
- **The 3.3 V supply is at 2.07 V at idle.** A 3.3 V regulator (U2, marked 6206A …33) should give 3.135 to 3.465 V. At 2.07 V the microcontroller is close to the voltage where it stops working, so any extra load (a button's LED, the motor) can reset it. With the power off the same supply reads 10 kΩ to ground, not a short: either U2 gets too little at its input, U2 itself is weak, or something on the 3.3 V supply draws current only once powered (an IC, a leaky D13). This reading assumes U2 pin 1 is ground, as the datasheet says; the next round checks it.
- **The three buttons open and close cleanly.** SW1 is Timer, SW2 Move and SW3 Shooting Star. Each reads 0 Ω pressed and OL or megohms released: the megohms are the microcontroller's input around the button, not a leak.
- **The 5 V supply reads 72 Ω to ground, power off.** Low for a supply, but not a short, and the same with the switch ON or OFF. Expected with the switch: with the adapter unplugged nothing else sits on the input side. Compared later with the powered readings.
- **U4's supply does not come from the DC+ pad.** 8.7 MΩ between U4 pin 4 and DC+ with the switch ON, as with it OFF (8.6 MΩ). U4 is fed another way: through a part, or from the third input wire (R1B). Left for later: U4 cannot explain the resets on Timer.
- **U2 has a good input and a low output.** 5.04 V reaches the board (R27) and 4.86 V reaches U2's input (R28), with U2 pin 1 confirmed as ground (R26). A 3.3 V regulator with 4.86 V in should give 3.3 V; it gives 2.07 V (R25). So the fault is U2 itself, or a load on its output that it cannot feed.
- **U2 stays cool at idle.** Nothing on the 3.3 V side feels warmer than the rest of the board after two minutes at idle. An overload strong enough to pull a 3.3 V regulator down to 2.07 V would make it hot: U2 is not feeding an overload, it is failing.
- **D13 is U2's protection diode.** Powered, D13's band end reads 4.84 V (U2's input) and its other end 2.09 V (U2's output). It runs from the 3.3 V side up to the 5 V side, the usual protection for a regulator. A leak there would raise the 3.3 V, not lower it: D13 is not the cause, and its low readings on the board (R13) came from U2 and the parts around it.
